## Module scope
No lectures and no readings this module. The stated work is to finalize the
visit to a rehabilitation center, complete any pre-visit requirements the
center imposes, and prepare a question list to share with peers and
instructors. The Design Project Description's *Rehabilitation Center Visit*
section is the reference.

**From the Design Project Description:**
- The visit gives perspective on patient demographics, clinician perspectives
  and the range of technologies in use.
- You interview a **care provider** (PT, clinician, nurse) about your selected
  project topic.
- Centers were to be identified in Module 1; plans solidified by Module 9; the
  visit happens in **Module 10**.
- During the visit: observe one patient in evaluation and treatment with the
  rehab team, experience assistive technology close to your project, discuss
  the project with staff and if possible clients, and take notes for the
  reflection.
- A 45–60 minute slot is suggested as a realistic ask.

## Deliverable
`Module_09_Assignment_Rehab_Center_Questions.docx` / `.pdf` — 2 pages,
**26 questions** against a required minimum of 10. Built by `doc.py`.

Covers all five categories the assignment names, in order:
- **A. State of the art** (5) — current pathway, what exists, cost and lead
  time, the widest gap, patient-built workarounds.
- **B. User needs and relative importance** (5) — explicitly asks the
  interviewee to rank essential / important / nice-to-have, which is what the
  assignment asks for and what Module 6's Kano work set up.
- **C. Design metrics** (5) — tipping limit and stability margin, sustained
  one-handed holding capacity, mass budget before propulsion degrades, real
  out-of-pocket price ceiling, policy and liability constraints.
- **D. Solution directions and concept feedback** (4) — three broad directions
  offered at a level general enough that the interviewee can reject all three
  and propose a fourth.
- **E. If a client is available** (4) — narrative rather than evaluative, since
  the assignment notes client and clinician needs commonly differ.
- **F. Specific to Kennedy Krieger** (3) — the therapy garden's gravel, grass
  and cobblestone surfaces; the age-21 transition and where patients get
  equipment afterward; and facility-side versus patient-carried solutions,
  using the aquatic center's lift floors as the example.

## Known tension with the facility
Kennedy Krieger is **pediatric**, treating individuals up to age 21, while the
project population is adults maintaining their own residential yard. Question
25 turns that into an asset by asking about the transition to independent
living and where patients source equipment after aging out, but expect it to
come up and have an answer ready. Sections A, B and C are written to be
answerable by any clinician about wheelchair users generally, so they survive
the mismatch; Section E's client questions are the ones most affected.

## Notes on the approach
- The assignment warns against arriving with a preconceived solution. Module 5
  deliberately proposed none, so Section D asks about **directions** rather
  than designs, and three of the questions (A4, D3, B3) are written to expose
  the possibility that the Module 5 scoping is wrong.
- Question style follows Module 8: ask what the clinician has **observed people
  do**, not what they think of the idea. "What made people stop using the last
  one?" returns data; "would you use this?" returns politeness.
- The visit is the first instalment of the evidence the Module 5 Phase 0 plan
  committed to gathering — eight manual wheelchair users plus two or three OTs
  or ATPs. Worth saying so in the submission if the continuity should be
  visible to the grader.
