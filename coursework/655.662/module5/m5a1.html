Physical Architecture — PD-RPM Remote Care System

An architecture is "the structure of components, their relationships, and the principles and guidelines governing their design and evolution over time." The physical architecture view expresses that structure as the physical elements of the system and their interfaces. Figure 1 is the PD-RPM physical architecture, organized by the four physical locations the system actually occupies, and corresponds to DoDAF SV-1 (systems interface description) and SV-2 (systems resource flow). The element inventory and interface matrix on page 2 complete it as an SV-6.

Figure 1. PD-RPM physical architecture (SV-1 / SV-2). Twenty physical elements in four locations; sixteen interfaces, each tagged with the identifier used in Table 2.

Principles governing design and evolution. Four rules constrain every element above. One, nothing inside the home makes a clinical judgment — HG-1 buffers and forwards, and all evaluation happens at RE-1 under version control. Two, every interface crossing a location boundary is mutually authenticated and encrypted, so adding a location adds no new trust assumption. Three, monitoring-center services are stateless behind the I-12 bus, so any one can be replaced without touching its neighbors. Four, CY-1 and EH-1 stay outside the boundary, so their vendors’ release cycles cannot stall this program.

On mental models. Drawing the physical view forced an assumption into the open that the functional view had concealed: I had been picturing "the monitoring center" as one thing. It is ten services with five distinct failure modes, and the patient cannot tell which one failed. Making the picture explicit is what let that be examined rather than carried.
