Risk R-1 — Silent Loss of Home Data Delivery, PD-RPM Remote Care System

Risk ID / titleR-1 — Silent Loss of Home Data Delivery
Risk statementIF the home gateway (HG-1) stops delivering completed treatment sessions to the monitoring center for more than 48 hours — through SIM deactivation, carrier outage, firmware fault, or exhaustion of the store-and-forward buffer — THEN no alert is raised for that patient, the care team reads continued silence as clinical stability, and a declining-ultrafiltration or early peritonitis trend progresses undetected until the next scheduled clinic contact.
DescriptionPD-RPM infers patient status from a continuous stream of nightly cycler sessions and daily patient entries. The architecture carries no positive indication that a quiet patient is a connected patient: RE-1 evaluates the records it receives and is silent when it receives none. A healthy patient and a dead link produce the same thing on the triage queue — nothing. This is the one failure mode the system is structurally unequipped to see, because every other failure announces itself as an alert.
Initial assessment Likelihood
4		 Across a 150-patient home census, carrier outages, SIM provisioning errors and firmware faults are routine rather than exceptional; the program should expect several silent gateways per month. RF = 4 × 4 = 16
High
Consequences if realized Consequence
4		 Loss of detection lead time, which is the clinical value the system exists to deliver. KPP-1 (alert latency ≤ 15 min) and KPP-3 (data completeness ≥ 95% within 24 h) are both void for the affected patient. A missed early-peritonitis window converts an outpatient antibiotic course into an admission and, in a meaningful fraction of cases, a permanent transfer to in-center hemodialysis.
Handling strategyControl / mitigation, with monitor on the residual. Avoidance would mean relaxing the requirement the program exists to meet. Transference moves carrier uptime to an SLA but leaves the clinical consequence with the provider. Acceptance is not available at RF 16, which the JHU ownership model places on the Risk Mitigation Execution List under continuous executive attention.

Residual risk and verification. After M-4 the risk stands at L2 × C2 = 4, Low. It is not closed: it moves off the Risk Mitigation Execution List onto the Risk Watch List under continuous monitoring, because both remaining drivers — carrier behaviour and firmware — sit outside this program’s control. Verification is the monthly fleet delivery-rate report measured against KPP-3; if any month falls below 95 percent, or if any single patient records two Device Silent conditions in a quarter, the risk is reopened at its original level rather than re-scored downward.

On mental models. The hard part of this risk was seeing it at all. Every other PD-RPM failure announces itself as an alert; this one is indistinguishable from success from inside the triage queue, and the dashboard actively reinforces the model that produces it — no alerts means no problem. M-1 is placed first in the sequence for that reason. It buys the smallest reduction in raw risk factor of the four activities, but it is the only one that makes the faulty model false by construction: after it, silence is itself an alert.
