## Deck content map

### Brain and creativity (slides 3–5)
- Thinking and design are **sciences of the artificial**.
- **Freud's levels of the mind:** conscious / preconscious / subconscious.
- **Brain-dominance theory** (slide 3 table):

  | Critical thinking (left brain) | Creative thinking (right brain) |
  |---|---|
  | Logical, analytic, judgmental | Generative, suspended judgment |
  | Linear | Associative |
  | Leads to only one solution | Creates many possible solutions |
  | Considers only relevant information | Considers broad range of information |
  | Sequential, rule-based movement | Random-pattern movement |
  | Embodies scientific principles | Heavily influenced by symbols and imagery |
  | Classifications and labels are rigid | Reclassifies objects to generate ideas |
  | Vertical | Lateral |
  | Convergent | Divergent |

- **Hawkins (slide 4):** the brain is a memory that stores patterns and a
  predictor — what we do next depends on the models we have stored.
- **Creativity (slide 5):** synthesizing new combinations of ideas into
  meaningful, useful forms. A creative engineer produces *a lot* of useful
  ideas. Flash-like spontaneity is a myth; the idea is initially only
  imperfectly understood.

### Supports to creative thinking (slide 6)
Develop a creative attitude · unlock your imagination · be persistent ·
develop an open mind · suspend your judgment · set problem boundaries.

### Four-stage model of creative thinking (slide 7)
1. **Preparation** — examine the problem elements and their interrelations.
2. **Incubation** — sleep on the problem; disengage the conscious mind.
3. **Inspiration** — a solution or a path toward one emerges.
4. **Verification** — check the inspired solution against the desired result.

### Mental blocks (slides 8–13)
A mental block prevents correct perception of a problem or conception of its
solution — it inhibits normal cognitive processes.

- **Perceptual:** stereotyping; information overload ("can't see the forest for
  the trees"); limiting the problem unnecessarily; fixation; priming/cues.
- **Emotional:** fear of risk taking; unease with chaos; inability or
  unwillingness to incubate; motivation.
- **Intellectual:** poor choice of problem-solving language or representation;
  memory block; insufficient knowledge base; incorrect information.
- **Environmental:** physical environment; criticism.

### Creative thinking methods (slides 14–23)
Methods target four problem-solver characteristics: **sensitivity** (recognize
a problem exists), **fluency** (many alternatives), **flexibility** (wide range
of approaches), **originality** (original solutions).

- **Brainstorming** — most common team method; a carefully orchestrated
  process, not any idea generation; want ideas from everyone.
- **SCAMPER checklist** (slide 17): Substitute · Combine · Adapt · Modify,
  magnify, minify · Put to other uses · Eliminate · Rearrange, reverse.
- **Refinement and evaluation** — the objective is *not* to winnow to one idea
  but to identify creative, feasible, practical ones. Quick sort: feasible as
  they stand / potential after more thought / unfeasible.
- **Beyond brainstorming** — Six Key Questions (who/what/when/where/why/how);
  Five Whys (deck's worked example: stopped machine → blown fuse → overloaded
  fan → inadequate bearing lubrication → stopped pump → shaft vibration from
  abrasion → no filter on the lube pump); checklists; fantasy/wishful thinking.
- **Technological stretching checklist** (slide 20, Thompson & London 1999):
  what happens if we push the conditions to the limit — temperature, pressure,
  concentration, impurities, up or down?
- **Synectics** (Gordon) — reasoning by analogy; four types: direct, fantasy,
  personal, symbolic.
- **Biomimetic design** — direct analogies from biological systems (Velcro).
  Two challenges: designers aren't trained broadly in biology, and engineering
  behavior vocabulary doesn't match biological vocabulary.
- **Concept map** — central problem label, surrounding thoughts circled and
  connected, arrows for what drives what, major branches for subtopics.

### Creative methods for design (slides 24–29)
- Quantity above quality early; wild ideas encouraged; sort out infeasible
  ideas afterward to find a smaller developable subset.
- **Systematic methods** for mechanical conceptual design: functional
  decomposition and synthesis · morphological analysis · design optimization ·
  decision-based design.
- **Functional decomposition** — define the system in functional terms, then
  lower-level functions and their sequencing. Each block describable without an
  "and" or an "or" → pure functions → reusable/replaceable, with simple generic
  interfaces. Mechanical design is **recursive**. The method is
  **solution-neutral**, which is what preserves creative leeway.

### Behavioral aspects of decision making (Ch. 7, slides 30–32)
Decision stress has two sources: fear of material/social loss from either
course, and reputation/self-esteem as a competent decision maker.

Five coping patterns — **all defective except the last**:
unconflicted adherence · unconflicted change · defensive avoidance ·
hypervigilance · **vigilance**.

Steps to a good decision: establish objectives → classify by importance →
develop alternatives → evaluate alternatives against objectives → tentative
decision (best promise of achieving all objectives) → explore for adverse
consequences → control effects with preventive actions and follow-through.
