Test and Assessment Approach — Peritoneal Dialysis Remote Patient Monitoring (PD-RPM)

1. Approach

The approach is organized on two axes. By level, testing is incremental: the same obligation is retested at five rising levels, each with more of the real system present. By path, the module’s own definition of integration demands that execution be checked on all nominal and exceptional paths, so two scenarios are carried end to end — one of each.

Scenario A is the nominal path and is run at every level, growing in fidelity from a unit test on one function to a clinical pilot. Scenario B is the exceptional path and exists only as a total-system test, because the failure it reproduces cannot be staged in any smaller piece of the system.

Both scenarios are assessed twice over: for functionality, meaning the system does what the specification says, and for utility, meaning a care team is measurably better off. A system can pass every functional test and still be useless, so the two are scored separately.

6. Scenario B — silent gateway across three Sunday nights (exceptional path, total system)

StepActionConditionExpected system response and pass criterion
1Establish baselineFull system live, 20 patients, 7 nights nominalEvery session ingested; no open alerts
2Inject the faultMains removed from HG-1 at 21:00 for 8 hours on 3 consecutive SundaysHG-1 transfers to battery and notifies DM-1 within 60 seconds
3Observe during outageSessions continue while the uplink is downCarrier link declared unavailable after 3 failed attempts in 5 minutes; all sessions buffered
4Detect the silenceNo data arrives from a patient who should be reporting nightlyA device-silent alert reaches the monitoring queue within 2 hours of the first missed session
5RestoreMains returnedEvery buffered record delivered in chronological order within 30 minutes; zero lost, zero duplicated
6AssessReview with nurse, firmware lead and carrier engineerThe care team learned of the gap from the system, not from the patient

Scenario B is not invented. It reproduces the phantom Sunday outage the Module 6 team-learning review uncovered, where eleven patients went silent for three consecutive Sunday nights and no team held enough evidence to see it. The decisive step is step 4: an alert on the absence of data. Nothing in the original failure detected that, and no test built only from the nominal path ever would.

8. Risk mitigation through the test program

RiskHow the approach mitigates it
R-1 silent gateway (M5A2)Scenario B is the direct test of it, including the data-absence alert that did not exist before.
False alerts exhausting the nursesScenario A at L3 bounds the non-actionable alert rate across 60 patient-months before any clinician sees the system.
External interfaces unavailable for testScenario A runs at L1–L4 against a cycler simulator and the EHR vendor sandbox, so neither external party gates the test program.
Late defects are expensiveThe same scenario repeats at every level, so a regression is caught at the lowest level that can expose it.

9. Why the approach is agile

Both scenarios are automated once they first pass, and then run as nightly regression for the rest of the program. That is what makes the incremental approach affordable: each increment re-runs the full scenario set overnight, and a failure is attributable to the increment that introduced it rather than hunted for at the end. Scenario A is re-run unchanged at every level, so the cost of re-testing falls as confidence rises.

10. Coverage, limits, and end-to-end assurance

Coverage is honest about its limits. Two scenarios do not test a system; they demonstrate that the approach reaches end to end on both the nominal and the exceptional path. Everything else is covered by the per-gate tests in the integration plan and by the verification cross-reference matrix, which is the artifact that proves no requirement is left without an owning test. The pilot at L5 is the only point at which PD-RPM claims to work rather than to have been built as specified.
